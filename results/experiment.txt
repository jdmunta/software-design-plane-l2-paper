FINDINGS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  * Search produced materially different architectures: mean pairwise mechanism distance 0.47, 5 distinct request paths across 5 candidates.
  * Evaluators disagree by 0.130 on average, most on 'performance' (0.287) — the dimension where structural and analytic reasoning genuinely diverge.
  * ['correctness'] carry no information about this candidate set — every evaluator returns a constant, so those dimensions neither separate candidates nor test evaluator independence.
  * 27% of candidate/dimension pairs are not separable under catalog resampling; the rest of the ordering survives it.
  * L2 rejected constraint violations that the human baseline carried into the design: ['inv_tenant_isolation']. These are structural property checks, not judgement calls.
  * Treatment vs baseline A — better on ['requirements satisfied', 'p99 latency', 'measured capacity']; worse on ['cost'].
  * Treatment vs baseline B — better on ['measured capacity']; worse on ['p99 latency', 'cost'].
  * NEGATIVE — human review cost scaled 5.0x: a reviewer must compare 5 frontier candidates rather than approve one. Any quality gain has to be worth that.

VERDICT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  SUPPORTED on this intent: multi-candidate search with independent evaluation improved the frontier without increasing unacceptable risk, at 5.0x review cost. 1 negative signal(s) remain.

CAVEATS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  * Every performance, cost and availability figure is produced by a model or a simulation in this harness, not by a deployed system. Section 14 of the paper calls for isolated real environments; substituting them changes the numbers but not the structure of the comparison.
  * Baseline A encodes the paper's own section 2 prescription. A different human baseline may change the result; pass baseline_a_overrides to test that.
  * Mechanism characteristics come from a hand-written catalog. The comparison is only as good as those estimates — which is why the separability finding above matters more than any single measurement.
